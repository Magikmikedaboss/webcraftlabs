<FieldNotebook author="Committee on Preservation Theory" institution="Internal Correspondence" date="Drafted Following Investigation 047">
We did not intend to write another Treatise. Investigation 047 made it unavoidable. For years we believed the difficult question was whether knowledge could survive. Now we face a stranger question. What if the evidence survives...but our assumptions about it do not?
</FieldNotebook>

<BigQuote>
Investigation 047 did not change the evidence. It changed how we think about evidence. This Treatise records the Committee's current understanding. Revision is expected.
</BigQuote>

<Chapter number={1} label="SECTION" eyebrow="Introduction" title="Why This Treatise Was Commissioned">

This edition is intentionally concise. It represents Revision 1.0 — the Committee's present thinking after reviewing the recovered investigations. The goal is not to deliver a final theory, but to record where we stand and to guide subsequent inquiry.

</Chapter>

<Chapter number={2} label="SECTION" eyebrow="Overview" title="Why Investigation 047 Changed Everything">

Investigation 047 reframed the problem: instead of asking whether information can survive, we must ask how we recognize information when familiar material carriers are absent or transformed. The point is not theoretical hair-splitting — it governs how evidence is collected, categorized, and trusted.

<HandwrittenNote>
This revision favors modesty. Where we once presumed clarity, we now prefer careful questions.
</HandwrittenNote>

</Chapter>

<Chapter number={3} label="SECTION" eyebrow="Concept" title="Evidence Is Not Proof">

<ThoughtExperiment title="A Footprint">
Imagine finding a footprint in fresh snow. The footprint is evidence. It tells you someone stood there. It does not tell you who. It does not tell you why. It does not tell you where they went. Evidence comes first. Meaning comes later.
</ThoughtExperiment>

Evidence is an invitation to inference, not a substitute for it. The Committee emphasizes careful separation between observation and interpretation: catalog what is present, document how it might have arisen, and only then evaluate competing explanations.

</Chapter>

<Chapter number={4} label="SECTION" eyebrow="Principles" title="Working Principles">

<LabObservation title="Current Committee Principles">
The Committee records the following working principles for Revision 1.0:

1. Preservation cannot be evaluated solely by the continued existence of material objects.

2. The absence of expected material evidence is not automatically evidence of historical absence.

3. Recovery and documentation must precede broad interpretive claims.

4. Preservation systems themselves may be historically significant even when their intended collections no longer survive.

5. Classification schemes are provisional and must be revised as new evidence arrives.

These principles guide the Committee's classifications and recommendations.
</LabObservation>

</Chapter>

<Chapter number={5} label="SECTION" eyebrow="Classification" title="The Four Forms of Evidence">

<LabObservation title="Current Committee Classification">
The Committee currently recognizes four provisional forms of evidence:

• Material — physical objects that can be directly examined.

• Informational — persistent patterns or encodings that survive across media.

• Contextual — relationships and provenance that situate observations.

• Interpretive — the explanations scholars construct from the foregoing.

These categories are provisional. Future investigations may require substantial revision.
</LabObservation>

<Insight title="Think About Music">
A melody can be played on a piano, a violin, or sung by a child. Every performance uses different matter, yet we still recognize the same song. Perhaps information survives in a similar way. That possibility is why Investigation 047 matters.
</Insight>

</Chapter>

<Chapter number={6} label="SECTION" eyebrow="Consensus" title="What We Believe Today">

<LabObservation title="Current Scholarly Positions">
Current scholarship agrees on the basic observations; it does not agree on their meaning. Some researchers argue that an earlier source document remains to be recovered. Others propose Investigation 047 reveals limits in our preservation models. The Committee does not presently endorse a single explanatory account; it records the range of credible positions and highlights where evidence is needed to adjudicate them.
</LabObservation>

</Chapter>

<Chapter number={7} label="SECTION" eyebrow="Outstanding" title="Questions That Remain">

Investigation 047 answered remarkably little but created better questions. The Committee lists the most urgent:

• Can information survive independently of its original material carrier?

• Does every recognizable pattern require an identifiable original copy?

• How should scholarship evaluate evidence that contradicts accepted theory?

• Which methods best detect informational continuity across media?

At present the Committee has no satisfactory answers. These questions will guide priorities for Revision 2.

</Chapter>
