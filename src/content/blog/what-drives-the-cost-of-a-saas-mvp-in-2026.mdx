"How much does a SaaS MVP cost?" doesn't have one answer, and it's worth understanding why before you ask anyone for a number.

Two products can both honestly be called an "MVP" and still have substantially different scope — different numbers of user roles, different data models, different integrations, different operational requirements. The word "MVP" tells you almost nothing about which one you're building. The scope does.

This guide walks through what actually drives that cost, so you can plan realistically before a scoping conversation — not after getting a quote that doesn't match what you expected.

---

## The Cost Drivers That Actually Matter

Our own [SaaS platform development process](/services/saas-platform-development) is built around the same handful of things that consistently move cost, regardless of who builds it:

### Authentication and account structure

How many ways can someone log in? Is there one account type, or several? A single login flow is simple. Multiple account types with different access levels are not — and this is usually decided earlier in the process than founders expect.

### Roles and permissions

This is one of the more commonly underestimated drivers. Going from "everyone sees the same thing" to "different people see different things based on who they are" touches your data model, your interface, and your testing — not just one screen.

### Data model complexity

How many distinct entities does your product actually need (customers, orders, projects, whatever's core to you), and how do they relate to each other? A simple, flat data model is inexpensive to build around. A data model with lots of relationships is not, even if the interface looks similarly simple on the surface.

### Payments and billing

None, one-time, or recurring subscription billing are three very different amounts of work — and subscription billing in particular brings its own edge cases (upgrades, downgrades, failed payments, cancellations).

### Admin and back-office tooling

Does your team need a dashboard to manage the product, or just your customers? An MVP that only serves customers is a different (usually smaller) scope than one that also needs internal tooling from day one.

### Integrations

Every third-party API your product depends on is real scope — not a checkbox. Each one has its own quirks, failure modes, and testing burden.

## What Doesn't Move the Price as Much as Founders Think

Visual polish is usually not the biggest lever. A clean, simple interface sitting on top of a simple data model is inexpensive. A polished, beautiful interface sitting on top of a complex data model is not — the complexity was always in the structure underneath, not what it looks like on screen. If a quote seems high and your product "looks simple," the data model and roles underneath it are usually why.

## Why We Won't Give You a Generic Price Table

You've probably seen articles that answer "how much does a SaaS MVP cost" with a specific-looking number or range. We're not going to do that here, and it's worth explaining why.

Generic price ranges are difficult to apply without knowing the product's scope, roles, data model, integrations, and operational requirements — and those are exactly the variables that differ between any two projects both labeled "MVP." A number without that context tells you less than it appears to.

What's actually useful is knowing which levers move your specific cost, so you can have a grounded conversation about your specific product — which is what the rest of this guide is for.
